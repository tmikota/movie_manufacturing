How to save money in production.

Animation production is notoriously unprofitable.

Let's look at some of the data from a public company like technicolor.

Their profit magins sit around 1-4%

That's no different than any studio i've ever been at.

Now let's look at their expenses as a % of revenue over a 3 year period.

These percentages also make complete sense to me and reflect everything i've seen in the last 20 years.

## Artists = 70% of a company's budget.

What do artists complain most about?

The pipeline.

What does that actually mean?

Well pipeline encompasses "All the stuff that can make my job flow better".  And as i've said before, it's a word that means everything and nothing simultaneously.

In our movie manufacturing context - the pipeline is the factory, but it's also the supply chain.   It's everything.

The thing i've been tracking for about 20 years now is how artists spend their time.

Broadly artists spend their time in one of a few ways:

Art

Technical Chores

Meetings

The question is how we define them.

We have a whole page dedicated to displaying how data flows in an animation production.   That same page shows the ratio of technical chores: art on any given production task.

Let's think about that for a second.

Artists are 70% of the budget of a studio.

This is the kind of work they engage in.

![](/images/essays/how-to-save-money-in-production/20261008-104352-8wza.png)

## Gold in your back yard

If THIS MUCH of an artist's time is spent on technical chores it stands to reasn that we'd want to eliminate them. So artists can do more art.   And that desire is what's led to this entire site, and the tools behind it that enable things like:

- How 1 image to a rig in unreal with 1 click.

What are we really talking about though - technical chores is pretty broad.
