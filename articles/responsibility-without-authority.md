<!-- Notes carried over from the DOWNTIME essay. -->

- At studios, the person responsible for something rarely has the authority to change it, and the person with the authority isn't the one responsible.
- A specialist knows what they need to do the job, usually a specific piece of software, and the studio says no. The reason is usually cost: the license, and the cost of rolling out something new across a studio.
- The person who has to deliver should get to choose. In a department that's the lead. Everyone in the department works toward what the lead delivers, and if the work doesn't land, it's on the lead. If they're responsible for the result, they need the authority to pick the tools.
- Production often treats artists as interchangeable, and inexperienced managers assign people as if any artist can do any job.
- It's why most of the eight wastes survive: nobody owns the 15-click workflow, the naming convention nobody wrote down, or the renders piling up on the server, so nobody can fix them.
